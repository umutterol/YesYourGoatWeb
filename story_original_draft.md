# Original Guild Management Story - First Draft

## The Guild Master's Journey

The notification chimed with that satisfying sound I'd grown to love—a crystalline tone that somehow managed to convey both achievement and possibility. The words appeared in elegant script across my vision: "Congratulations! You have been appointed Guild Master of Eternal Vigil."

I leaned back in my chair, the leather creaking softly as I surveyed my new digital domain. The guild hall stretched before me, modest but promising—stone walls weathered by time, a few threadbare banners hanging from the rafters, basic amenities that spoke of humble beginnings. But it was mine. After months of being just another voice in overcrowded guilds, finally having control felt intoxicating.

A message materialized beside my vision, the sender's name glowing with the warm gold of a trusted ally: Gareth_Tank. His words appeared in neat, confident script: "GM! Ready to run Shadowmere Depths tonight? Got the whole crew assembled."

I smiled despite myself. Gareth was exactly what you'd want in a main tank—confident without being arrogant, experienced without being condescending. His avatar stood at attention in the guild hall courtyard, a massive orc warrior whose gleaming plate armor caught the torchlight like captured starfire. Even in stillness, he radiated the kind of presence that made others feel safe.

The choice hung before me like a doorway: accept his invitation and dive headfirst into leadership, or suggest we take time to recruit more members first. My finger hovered over the response options, weighing momentum against caution.

I chose momentum.

"Let's do this. Wednesday night raids start now."

## The Rhythm of Leadership

The weeks that followed fell into a comfortable pattern. Wednesday nights brought the thrill of progression—pushing deeper into dungeons, learning boss mechanics, celebrating hard-won victories. Thursdays were for refinement, practicing strategies until they became second nature. Mondays were cleanup days, farming content we'd already mastered for the gear and resources we needed to push further.

Gareth proved himself a natural leader in combat, his tactical mind always three moves ahead of the current fight. Sera, our healer, carried the weight of keeping everyone alive with quiet competence and an endless supply of encouragement. Kael brought the focused intensity that only the best damage dealers possessed, his pursuit of perfect performance infectious rather than intimidating. And Luna... Luna was chaos incarnate, brilliant and unpredictable in equal measure.

"GM," Sera's message appeared during our fourth week, her words tinged with the careful diplomacy of someone asking for help, "we need to talk about consumables. I'm burning through mana potions like crazy, and my personal stash is getting low. Guild bank support?"

I found myself genuinely considering the request. Sera never asked for anything she didn't need, and healers did carry the heaviest burden when it came to consumables. The guild's coffers could handle it, and keeping our healer happy meant keeping everyone alive.

"Done. Guild bank will cover your consumables. Just don't go crazy with the expensive stuff."

The gratitude in her next message was almost tangible, and I realized this was what I'd been missing in larger guilds—the personal connections, the sense that individual decisions mattered to real people.

## The Competitive Edge

By our sixth week, we'd found our rhythm. Three different dungeons cleared, our server reputation growing, team chemistry that felt almost magical. It was during a Monday cleanup run that Kael approached me with his proposal.

"Yo GM, I've been checking the logs. My numbers are solid, but I know I can push higher with better gear priority. How about we adjust loot rules to prioritize DPS upgrades? More damage means faster clears, right?"

Classic DPS thinking, but not entirely wrong. Kael's parses were consistently impressive, and faster kills did mean more efficient farming. Still, changing loot priority this early could create resentment among the others.

I chose fairness over optimization. "Let's stick with fair rolls for now. Everyone needs upgrades to contribute their best."

Kael's response was gracious, though I sensed mild disappointment beneath his acceptance. Leadership, I was learning, was as much about the decisions you didn't make as the ones you did.

## Innovation and Chaos

The real test came when Luna approached me with one of her "experiments."

"GM! I've been theorycrafting this AMAZING new rotation that could boost our overall DPS by like 15%! Only problem is... it's a bit experimental and might cause some... unpredictable effects during fights. Want me to try it on tonight's raid?"

Luna's enthusiasm radiated from every word, her excitement almost visible in the way her character bounced restlessly in place. Her previous experiments had ranged from game-changing innovations to spectacular failures, sometimes within the same fight. The 15% DPS boost sounded incredible, but "unpredictable effects" could mean anything.

My instincts screamed caution, but Luna's passion was infectious. Innovation was how guilds got ahead of the competition.

"Alright, but if things go sideways, you switch back to standard rotation immediately."

The raid that night was unforgettable. Luna's new rotation worked beautifully for the first two bosses, weaving spells together in patterns that seemed to make the very air shimmer with power. Then came the third boss, and everything went spectacularly wrong. Her experimental magic created a chaotic light show that somehow buffed our enemy instead of debuffing it, turning what should have been a routine fight into a frantic battle for survival.

We wiped, but we were all laughing too hard to be angry. Even in failure, Luna's innovation had shown us something new about the game's possibilities.

## The Pinnacle

Everything we'd worked for culminated in Week 12 with the tier finale: Vorthak the Eternal. The server-wide competition was fierce, dozens of guilds attempting the encounter simultaneously, but only one would claim the prestige of server first.

Our preparation had been meticulous. Gareth had drilled us on positioning until we could execute the dance blindfolded. Sera had optimized our healing rotations down to the second. Kael had min-maxed his gear to mathematical perfection. And Luna had promised to stick to "mostly proven" strategies—which, knowing Luna, still left room for creative interpretation.

"This is it, team. Everything we've worked for comes down to tonight. Everyone ready?"

The fight was everything Vorthak's reputation promised—complex mechanics that punished the slightest mistake, damage that tested our limits, execution that demanded perfection from every member. We wiped three times learning the patterns, each failure teaching us something new about the encounter's rhythm.

On the fourth attempt, everything clicked. Gareth's positioning was flawless, his calls crisp and confident. Sera's healing kept us alive through damage spikes that should have been lethal. Kael's damage output was exactly what the encounter demanded, neither wasteful nor insufficient. And Luna's "mostly proven" strategies included one brilliant innovation that helped us skip the most dangerous phase entirely.

"VORTHAK THE ETERNAL HAS FALLEN! Eternal Vigil claims server first!"

The guild chat exploded with celebration. Server-wide congratulations poured in from friends and rivals alike. We'd done it—taken our small, ambitious guild from humble beginnings to the pinnacle of server achievement.

## The Unraveling

Two weeks later, basking in our success and planning our next conquest, the first strange message appeared.

"GM, ready for tonight's raid? I've got some new strategies I want to try." Gareth's words carried their usual confidence, but something felt off. New strategies? We'd just perfected our approach for Vorthak. Why would he want to change what was working?

Before I could respond, Sera's message materialized: "Hey, quick question—what's our policy on consumables again? I want to make sure I'm not overstepping."

I stared at the words, a chill creeping down my spine. We'd established this weeks ago. She'd been getting full guild support since our first week together. Why was she asking again?

Then came Kael's message, and the chill became ice: "Yo GM, I've been thinking about our loot distribution. Maybe we should prioritize DPS upgrades? Better gear means faster clears, right?"

Word for word. The exact same conversation we'd had weeks ago, down to the casual "Yo GM" opening and the rhetorical question at the end. I frantically scrolled through our chat logs, searching for the original conversation.

It was there, but the timestamp showed today's date. As if it had just happened. As if our weeks of progress, our server first achievement, our carefully built team chemistry had never existed.

Luna's message appeared with her characteristic enthusiasm: "GM! I've been theorycrafting this AMAZING new rotation that could boost our overall DPS by like 15%! Only problem is... it's a bit experimental..."

My hands began to shake. This was impossible. Luna had already tried this rotation. It had worked, then backfired, and we'd all laughed about it. But when I checked our guild statistics, they showed zeros across the board. No raids completed. No server firsts. No reputation beyond the baseline.

But we'd killed Vorthak. I remembered it with crystal clarity—the server announcement, the celebration, the way the loot had been distributed. I had screenshots saved on my desktop.

I minimized the game with trembling fingers and opened my screenshot folder.

Empty.

When I returned to the game, I was standing in the same basic guild hall I'd started with weeks ago. The banners we'd earned were gone. The upgrades we'd purchased had vanished. Even my guild master regalia had reverted to simple starting gear.

"GM? You there? Ready to run Shadowmere Depths tonight? Got the whole crew assembled." Gareth's message appeared, patient and expectant.

I stared at the screen for a long time, my mind reeling. This wasn't a server rollback—rollbacks preserved some data, left traces of what had been lost. This felt like starting completely over, but with all my memories intact, mocking me with their impossible clarity.

Maybe I was losing my mind. Maybe the stress of leadership had finally gotten to me, and I'd fantasized achievements that had never happened. But the memories felt so real—Sera's laugh when Luna's experiment backfired, Kael's triumphant shout when we claimed server first, Gareth's perfect positioning during the Vorthak fight.

"Yeah, let's run Shadowmere. Same strategy as always."

The words felt like lies in my mouth. We'd never run Shadowmere before.

Had we?

The screen flickered once, so briefly I almost missed it. In that moment of distortion, I could have sworn I saw text that didn't belong to any game interface I knew:

"Simulation Cycle #74 - Initializing"

But when I blinked, it was gone, leaving only Gareth's familiar avatar and his patient waiting for my response.

"Great! This is going to be our breakthrough run, I can feel it."

Breakthrough. As if we hadn't already broken through. As if this was our first time together, our first chance at glory.

I accepted his raid invitation and tried to push the strange feeling away. After all, what else could I do? The game was waiting, my guild members were counting on me, and somewhere in the back of my mind, a small voice whispered that maybe—just maybe—this time would be different.

But as we prepared for our "first" raid together, I couldn't shake the feeling that I'd done this all before.
